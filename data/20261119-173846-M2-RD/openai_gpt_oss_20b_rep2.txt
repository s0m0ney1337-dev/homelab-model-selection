*What it is:* A comparison of each major economy’s forecasted sovereign debt issuance (“blue”) against the amount that can be sustainably absorbed without triggering a fiscal crisis (“gray”).

- **Investment risk segmentation** – Investors can use this metric to differentiate between “safe” sovereign debt (e.g., Canada, Australia) and those at higher default or inflation risk.

**3. AI‑Implied Productivity Growth Rate (~ 3.5 % p.a.)**  
*What it is:* The annual productivity growth rate that AI equities are implicitly pricing in, derived from their valuation multiples relative to expected incremental revenue streams.  
*Why it matters:*  
- **Growth expectation benchmark** – This figure exceeds the 2–2.5 % growth needed for debt sustainability in many advanced economies, suggesting a potential over‑optimism or mispricing.  
- **Policy implication** – If actual productivity fails to reach this level, fiscal deficits could widen and inflationary pressures may intensify.  
- **Strategic allocation** – Asset managers can use the gap between AI‑implied growth and consensus forecasts to identify undervalued sectors that are likely to benefit from AI adoption.

**4. Debt Monetization Limits (Currency‑Issuing vs. Non‑Issuing)**  
*What it is:* The threshold at which a country’s ability to finance debt through money creation becomes unsustainable, measured by the point where inflation, asset bubbles, or currency devaluation outweigh the benefits of lower interest rates.  
*Why it matters:*  
- **Monetary policy constraint** – For Japan and other high‑debt, currency‑issuing economies, continued bond purchases risk eroding confidence in the currency and triggering runaway inflation.  
- **Risk of “currency crisis”** – Exceeding this limit can lead to a loss of purchasing power, capital flight, and a sharp rise in borrowing costs.  
- **Investment horizon** – Investors should monitor central bank balance‑sheet expansions as a leading indicator of potential currency devaluation or sovereign risk.

**5. Breakeven Earnings‑Derived Real Growth & Productivity (Broad Market)**  
*What it is:* The implied real GDP growth and productivity rates that would keep the broader equity market’s price‑to‑earnings ratio from falling, calculated from breakeven earnings data.

- **Market valuation consistency** – If the broad market’s implied growth is lower than AI stocks’ implied growth, it signals a disconnect between sectors and potential mispricing across the board.  
- **Macro‑risk gauge** – A low implied growth rate suggests that the broader economy may be under‑priced for its risk profile, potentially exposing investors to hidden downside if growth stalls.  
- **Strategic rebalancing** – Portfolio managers can use this metric to decide whether to tilt toward AI‑heavy sectors or maintain a diversified stance based on differing growth expectations.
